# What would strengthen this paper

Assessment written alongside the first draft (`manuscript_draft_JAC.md`) and
the first figures. Ordered by how much each would change a referee's
verdict, not by effort.

---

## The one thing the paper currently lacks

**A real measured dataset.** Every number in the draft comes from synthetic
data generated by the same model being fitted, so it is self-consistent by
construction and demonstrates nothing about the physics. A JAC referee will
ask for this, and rightly.

One good SANS dataset would exercise the whole chain at once -- loader,
resolution, form factor, closure, structure factor, fit -- and would let
several separate claims be made in a single figure. A polydisperse charged
colloid at two or three concentrations is the natural choice: at low
concentration S(Q) -> 1 and the fit determines the size distribution alone;
raising the concentration then tests the structure factor with the size
already fixed. That is a much stronger argument than fitting one curve, and
it needs no new code.

## What would most strengthen the science

### 1. Finish the section 5.3 survey

Currently four state points, which is an anecdote. But the anecdote is
striking: the best approximation scheme FLIPS between potentials by an order
of magnitude in both directions.

| system | decoupling | local monodisperse |
|---|---|---|
| hard sphere, phi=0.30, s=0.20 | **0.11** | 0.74 |
| square well, phi=0.20, s=0.30 | 2.16 | **0.07** |

A systematic sweep over s (0.05-0.4), phi (0.05-0.4) and three or four
potentials turns that into the paper's central result: a practical map of
which scheme is safe where. It is embarrassingly parallel -- every point is
an independent solve -- so it is an overnight batch job, not weeks of work.

**Present it as a heat map** of max error per scheme over the (s, phi) plane,
one panel per potential. That single figure would be the most useful thing in
the paper for a practising scatterer, and is the sort of thing that gets
cited.

### 2. State the cost honestly, and in a table

The paper's implicit claim is "the exact treatment is now cheap enough to be
the default". That claim is currently unsupported by any timing. Measure on a
stated machine: time per solve versus number of size classes and closure, and
time for a full fit. If a three-parameter fit really is ~30 solves and ~25 s,
say so -- it is a strong argument, and vague phrasing wastes it.

### 3. Show a case where the approximations visibly fail

The error plots are quantitative but abstract. One figure showing I(Q) from a
decoupling approximation and the exact result diverging *visibly* -- at high
polydispersity and volume fraction, where the difference is a factor of two
rather than 10 % -- makes the point to a reader who skims figures.

## What would most strengthen the methods section

### 4. The resolution result is publishable in itself

Measured, on smeared synthetic data with a true srel of 0.15:

| | chi2_red | fitted srel |
|---|---|---|
| without smearing | 1.073 | 0.1726 (15 % high) |
| with smearing | 0.750 | 0.1497 (0.2 % high) |

The point that carries: **chi2_red = 1.07 looks perfectly acceptable while
the answer is 15 % wrong**. That is a cautionary result about a mistake that
is easy to make and hard to notice, and it belongs in the paper regardless of
everything else. It also justifies the software's design rather than merely
describing it.

### 5. The correlation matrix, not just error bars

    meanRadius  1.000  -0.224   0.751
    srel       -0.224   1.000   0.389
    phi         0.751   0.389   1.000

phi and R correlate at 0.75. Reporting this makes the paper more honest than
most fitting papers: it says *which* parameters the data actually determines,
rather than quoting three numbers with error bars as if they were
independent. It costs nothing -- the Jacobian is already computed.

### 6. Validation belongs in a table, not prose

Scattered through the draft are: D'Aguanno & Klein reproduced to 0.01 % and
0.02 %; analytic Percus-Yevick hard spheres to 3.8 % and 1.9 % (grid);
the PY thermodynamic inconsistency reproduced as +1.33 against +1.25;
four bridge functions agreeing with an independent Julia implementation to
1e-16; the one-component reduction bit-identical; extended RY reducing to RY
to 2.5e-16; a form-factor amplitude matching analytic to ratio
1.0000000000. Collected into one table these are a strong statement about the
implementation. Scattered in prose they read as anecdotes.

## What would strengthen it for a JAC readership specifically

### 7. Say what a user should actually do

JAC's Computer Programs section rewards practical guidance. The paper knows
several things a practitioner needs and currently buries them:

* three moment-matched size classes suffice for the structure factor, but the
  form-factor average needs ~40 -- with the rule of thumb
  nFF >~ Qmax*sigma*s;
* a small residual does not mean a correct solve, since the closure equations
  admit multiple fixed points (two solvers converged to residuals of 1e-12
  and returned S(Q) = 17.0 and 9.7, both with min S < 0);
* solver convergence flags cannot be trusted (KINSOL reported success after
  diverging to ~1e217).

A short "practical recommendations" subsection would be among the most cited
parts of the paper.

### 8. Comparison with existing tools

Referees will ask how this differs from SasView, SASfit's own analytic
structure factors, and the various Ornstein-Zernike codes. A brief, fair
paragraph -- what each does well, what is genuinely new here -- pre-empts the
question. The honest claim is not "better" but "exact where others
approximate, and general where others are model-specific".

## What to leave out

* **The one-shot (Gamma, p) fixed-point idea.** Interesting, arguably a
  paper of its own, but unimplemented and unvalidated. Mentioning it as
  future work is fine; making anything of it is not.
* **Optimiser benchmarking.** least_squares 36 evaluations against DFO-LS 94
  and bumps amoeba 158 is a real measurement, but on one synthetic problem
  with three parameters. Too thin to publish, and a distraction from the
  physics. Keep it in the documentation.
* **The bumps de/dream back end**, until it has been run to completion at
  least once. It is currently untested plumbing.

---

## Honest self-assessment of the draft

The draft asserts things the work has not yet established. Specifically:

* Section 5.3 is written as though the survey exists. It does not.
* The introduction's literature framing reflects the papers consulted during
  development, not a survey, and should not pass for a considered review.
* Timings are quoted informally and were never measured on a stated machine.
* "The exact treatment is now cheap enough to be the default" is the paper's
  thesis and is currently supported by assertion rather than by a timing
  table.

Any of these would be reasonable for a referee to challenge. Better to fix
them than to have them found.
